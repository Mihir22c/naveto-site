Privacy Policy for Naveto

Last updated: 28 October 2026

Naveto is an app for learning German, published by an individual developer. This policy explains exactly what the app does with information, in plain language. The short version: you can use all of Naveto without an account, and then your learning stays on your phone. If you choose to create a profile, your name, email address and a copy of your progress are stored on the developer's server so you can continue on another phone. If you send a report from the app, or feedback from this website, it is stored so it can be read and answered. Each case is described below.

Using Naveto without an account

A profile is optional. Without one, Naveto never asks for your name, email address, phone number or date of birth, and your learning data stays on your device (see the next section). Nobody, including the developer, can look up what you answered or how you are progressing.

What is stored on your device

Your learning data is written to storage on your phone that belongs to the app alone. This includes:

  • Which lessons you have completed and your review schedule
  • Which grammar mistakes you have made, so the app can bring them back
  • Your settings: explanation language, sound, reminders, theme
  • Which days you studied, for your weekly target

Uninstalling the app deletes all of it from your device.

What can leave your phone

1. Speech recognition, only while you use the speaking exercise

Some exercises invite you to say a German sentence out loud. When you tap the microphone button, Naveto asks Android's own speech recognition service to listen and return text. On most phones this service is provided by Google, and your speech may be sent to Google's servers to be turned into text, under Google's privacy policy rather than this one.

On an iPhone, Naveto uses Apple's speech recognition. Where the iPhone supports it, the speech is turned into text on the phone itself and does not leave it; otherwise it is sent to Apple to be turned into text, under Apple's privacy policy. iOS asks your permission for speech recognition and for the microphone the first time.

Naveto itself never records, stores or uploads audio. It receives only the text of what the recogniser heard, compares it with the expected sentence, and discards it. The microphone is used only in the moments you tap that button, never in the background. Your phone asks your permission the first time, and declining is fine: the exercise falls back to marking yourself.

2. Backup of your progress, if your device does it

Naveto allows Android's standard backup system to include your learning progress, so that a new or reset phone can restore where you were. If Android Backup is switched on for your device, that data is copied to your own Google Drive account, where it counts against your storage and is governed by Google's terms. It is your backup, in your account; the developer has no access to it.

You can turn this off in your phone's own settings, usually under System → Backup. You can also export a backup file yourself from Profile → Your progress, which puts a copy wherever you choose. Files you export are entirely under your control.

3. Crash reports

Naveto uses Firebase Crashlytics, a Google service, to report crashes. When the app crashes it sends a diagnostic report so the fault can be fixed. A report contains the technical circumstances of the crash: the point in the code where it happened, your device model, operating system version, app version, and a randomly generated installation identifier that distinguishes one installation from another. It does not contain your name, your email, your learning data or anything you typed.

4. Purchases

Naveto sells nothing at the moment: every level, lesson and practice paper is free, and the app never asks you to buy anything. If purchases are added later, this section will say what they send and to whom before they appear in the app.

5. Written answers in the practice exams

The practice exams include written tasks. You mark your own answer against the criteria shown on screen, and that marking happens entirely on your device. Nothing you write in an exercise or an exam leaves your phone.

6. Your profile and synced progress, only if you create a profile

You can create a profile with an email address and password (and, where the app offers it, with Sign in with Apple or Google). If you do, the following is stored on the developer's server:

  • your email address, and your name if you enter one;
  • a copy of your learning progress: the same data listed under "What is stored on your device", including your settings and goals such as your target level and exam month;
  • which kind of phone saved that copy (for example "iPhone" or the model name of an Android phone) and when, so the app can ask which copy to keep when two phones disagree;
  • a user ID that the server creates for you.

Your password is stored only as a cryptographic hash by the sign-in service and cannot be read by anyone, including the developer. If you sign in with Apple or Google, Naveto receives your email address (or Apple's private relay address) and an identifier from them, never your Apple or Google password.

The purpose is to give you your progress back on another phone, and to let you see and continue it under your name. It is never used for advertising, never sold and never shared, apart from the service providers named under "Where the data is kept".

7. Reports and suggestions you send

You can report a problem, a wrong translation or an idea from inside the app. A report contains what you write, plus the context needed to find the problem: the screen, the lesson and sentence it is about and the text that was shown, your explanation and app languages, the app and content version, your phone's operating system and the text size you use. It is stored on the developer's server so it can be read, fixed and answered, and you can see the status and any reply in the app.

If you send a report without a profile, the server creates an anonymous user ID for you so that you can see your own reports. It contains no name or email. If you later create a profile on the same phone, your reports move to it. Please do not write personal information into a report that you would not want the developer to read.

You can add up to three screenshots or screen recordings to a report, picked by you from your photos (Naveto needs no access to your photo library for this; your phone's own picker hands over only what you choose). They are stored privately with the report, seen only by the developer, and deleted after 90 days. Check that a picture shows nothing private, such as other people's names, messages or notifications. The report also carries what the screen knew when you tapped Report, for example the task and the corrections of a talk with the speaking partner, so the problem can be found without a picture.

8. Feedback you send from this website

The feedback page on this website lets testers and learners send a weekly check-in, a problem or an idea without an account. It stores what you write and, only if you fill them in, your first name, your email address, your explanation language and your phone model, together with the time it was sent. The page records nothing else: no cookies, no device identifiers, no IP address in the stored message. It is read by the developer to improve Naveto and, if you left an email address, to answer you.

You can add up to three screenshots or screen recordings to it. They are stored privately with your message, seen only by the developer, and deleted after 90 days.

9. Speaking with Lena, the AI partner, only if you use it

"Speak with a partner" lets you practise the speaking tasks of the A2 and B1 exams with Lena, a practice partner run by an AI model. It needs a profile and an internet connection, and nothing happens unless you start a talk.

What you say is first turned into text by your phone's own speech recognition, as described in section 1. No recording of your voice is sent to Naveto. During a talk, the text of the conversation so far is sent with each turn to the developer's server in Frankfurt, which passes it to Anthropic (Anthropic, PBC, United States), whose Claude model writes Lena's answers, hints and the corrections at the end. Lena's answers are turned into speech by Google Cloud Text-to-Speech. Under their commercial terms neither provider uses this text to train its models; Anthropic may keep it for a limited time to detect abuse.

Naveto's server does not store the conversation. It keeps one row per talk: your user ID, which task it was, when it started, how many times you spoke, how many hints you asked for and an estimate of what the talk cost. That row is used to apply the limit on talks per week and the monthly budget, and is deleted with your account, or after 400 days.

If you report one of Lena's answers, the conversation up to that point is sent as part of the report (section 7), because the report is about it.

Where the data is kept

Profiles, synced progress, reports and website feedback are stored with Supabase (Supabase, Inc.), in its data centre in Frankfurt, Germany (EU). Supabase hosts the database and the sign-in service for the developer and processes the data only on the developer's instructions, under a data processing agreement that includes the EU Standard Contractual Clauses for any access from outside the EU. Sign in with Apple and Google sign-in, where used, are provided by Apple and Google under their own privacy policies.

All of this data travels encrypted (HTTPS). Each person can read and change only their own profile, progress and reports; this is enforced by the database itself, not only by the app.

How long it is kept, and deleting it

  • Profile and synced progress: until you delete your account. One copy of your progress is kept; each upload replaces the previous one.
  • Reports: while your account exists, so that you can see the answer, and deleted with it. Reports from a guest with no profile are deleted after 24 months.
  • Feedback from this website: 12 months, then deleted automatically. To have it deleted sooner, email the address under Contact, saying roughly when you sent it and what it said.
  • Deleting your account: in the app, under You → your profile → Delete account. This deletes your profile, the synced progress and your reports from the server immediately; nothing is kept in a "deactivated" state. Progress on your phone stays until you uninstall the app. If you cannot use the app, email the address under Contact from the address you signed up with, and the account will be deleted within 30 days.
  • The server's own backups, where they exist, are overwritten within 7 days.

Notifications

If you switch study reminders on, Naveto schedules them on your device. The app also registers with Firebase Cloud Messaging, a Google service, which issues a token identifying this installation so a notification could be delivered to it. Notifications are off until you turn them on, and Android asks your permission at that moment rather than at launch. You can turn them off again at any time in the app or in your phone's settings.

What Naveto does not do

  • It shows no advertising and contains no advertising software.
  • It has no analytics or tracking beyond the crash reporting described above. It does not record which screens you visit or how long you use it.
  • It does not sell or share your information with anyone. The only third parties involved are Google and Apple in the roles above; Supabase, which stores profiles, synced progress, reports and website feedback for the developer; and, only while you talk with the AI partner, Anthropic and Google Cloud Text-to-Speech (section 9).
  • It does not require an account. Every lesson works without one.
  • It does not use advertising identifiers, and does not track you across other apps or websites.

Children

Naveto is intended for adults and older teenagers learning German. It is not directed at children, contains no content aimed at children, and does not knowingly collect information from them. You need to be at least 16 to create a profile; younger learners can use every lesson without one.

Your rights

Without a profile, the app holds no personal data on any server. With one, you can see and correct your name in the app, take a copy of your progress (You → Save a backup), and delete everything (see "Deleting your account" above). You can also ask for a copy of everything stored about you, or ask any question, at the contact address below. In practice:

  • To erase everything: uninstall the app. To also remove the cloud copy, delete the app's backup in your Google Drive backup settings.
  • To stop the microphone being used: revoke the microphone permission in Android or iPhone settings, or simply do not use the speaking exercise.
  • To stop notifications: turn reminders off in the app, or block notifications for Naveto in Android settings.

If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom: your profile and synced progress are processed to provide the service you asked for (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR); reports and website feedback are processed at your request and in the legitimate interest of fixing and improving the app (Art. 6(1)(b) and (f)); crash reporting is carried out on the basis of legitimate interest in keeping the app working; and speech recognition and the AI partner are used at your request when you use those features (Art. 6(1)(b)). Sending the text of a talk to Anthropic in the United States relies on the European Commission's standard contractual clauses in Anthropic's data processing terms. You also have the right to complain to a data protection authority. You can exercise your rights, or ask any question about this policy, using the contact address below.

Changes to this policy

If the app's handling of information changes, this page will be updated and the date at the top will change with it. Substantial changes will also be noted in the app's release notes on Google Play.
